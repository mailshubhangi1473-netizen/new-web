Colorectal Cancer

Awareness, Prevention, and Early Detection

Definition Causes Symptoms Effects

Definition

Colorectal cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the colon or rectum, which are parts of the large intestine. It often starts as small growths called polyps that can develop into cancer over time.

If not detected early, colorectal cancer can grow and spread to other parts of the body, making treatment more difficult.

Causes and Risk Factors

Several factors increase the risk of colorectal cancer:

  • Age: Risk increases after age 50.
  • Diet: High intake of red and processed meats.
  • Lifestyle: Lack of physical activity, obesity, smoking, alcohol.
  • Family History: Genetic predisposition.
  • Medical Conditions: Inflammatory bowel diseases like Crohn’s disease.
  • Polyps: Previous colon polyps increase risk.

Symptoms

Common symptoms include:

  • Changes in bowel habits (diarrhea or constipation)
  • Blood in stool
  • Abdominal pain or cramps
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Fatigue and weakness
  • Feeling that the bowel does not empty completely

Symptoms may not appear in early stages, making regular screening important.

Effects and Complications

Colorectal cancer can lead to several complications:

  • Blockage: Tumor may block the colon or rectum.
  • Spread (Metastasis): Can spread to liver, lungs, and other organs.
  • Bleeding: Leading to anemia.
  • Treatment Side Effects: Surgery, chemotherapy, radiation.
  • Emotional Impact: Stress, anxiety, and lifestyle changes.

Early screening such as colonoscopy can help detect and prevent colorectal cancer.
